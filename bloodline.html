The Saga

One Spark, Ten Thousand Years

The world has ended before. It will end again. The only thing that has ever crossed from one world to the next is you.

You wake at the dawn of everything, in a body that will not last. You learn to make fire. You learn the words that keep a tribe alive through the long dark. You bury the ones who came before you, and you raise the ones who come after. Then you grow old, and you feel the cold reach your chest, and you close your eyes.

And you open them again, looking out from your own child.

This is the secret the game is built on. You are not a hero. You are a thread of blood and memory that refuses to be cut. Every life you live is borrowed, and every death hands you forward to the next. What carries across is small and precious: a gift written into the body, a skill burned into the hands, a hard lesson your grandmother paid for in full.

The ages turn whether you are ready or not. The ice comes. The waters rise. The old world breaks and a new one takes its place, and the things that kept your line alive yesterday are useless against tomorrow. You survive by building. The shelter against the storm. The walls that keep your children breathing long enough to inherit. The knowledge you pass down before the body that holds it falls.

"Some men plant trees they will never sit beneath. You plant the whole forest, then live to walk in it as your own descendant."

How It Plays

The Rules of the Line

I

The Ages · Timeline Advances

  • The world moves through ages: Dawn, Stone, Bronze, Iron, and on toward the present and past it.
  • You never choose when an age ends. A time-shift arrives as a world-rewriting cataclysm: an ice age, a great flood, a collapse.
  • Each shift tests your line. Reach the threshold of tech, shelter, and knowledge and you cross over. Fall short and the cataclysm takes everything you built.
  • Every new age rewrites the map, the resources, and the threats. What saved you last age may be worthless in the next.
  • Crossing is permanent. The world you knew is gone, and the line carries on into a stranger one.
II

Death · The Handoff

  • Every body is mortal. Each character ages on a lifespan clock and will die. This is the engine of the game, not a penalty.
  • When your body dies of age, control passes into your living heir. You wake as them and the story continues unbroken.
  • Death by starvation, cold, wound, or cataclysm is failure, and it can cost you the line.
  • Die with no heir and no way to continue the blood, and the line ends. That is a true ending.
  • A life leaves a mark. The skills a body mastered and the traits it carried become candidates to pass down.
III

The Line · Reproducing

  • To outlive a single body, you must continue the blood. Raise a family and bring up heirs before death comes for you.
  • Who you pair with matters. A mate carries their own traits, and the union shapes what your children can inherit.
  • Children grow in real time. A line with no next generation ready is a line one bad winter from ending.
  • Heirs are real people in the world long before you inherit them: raised, fed, protected, and taught.
  • Multiple children let you choose which one to continue as, each a different inherited hand.Vision
IV

Knowledge · Tech Advancement

  • Knowledge is the spine of survival: fire, tools, language, farming, metalwork, and up the long ladder.
  • Tech is earned in the world and recorded to the line. It carries across generations through what you choose to pass down.
  • Each age has its own ceiling and its own unlocks. The cataclysm threshold is, above all, a test of how far you've climbed.
  • Lose the carrier of a skill before it's passed on, and that knowledge can be lost to your line. Progress can break.
  • Tech and blood are linked. Some advances only open to a lineage that carries the traits to support them.
V

Genetic Crafting · The Signature

  • Your bloodline is something you craft, the same way you craft a blade or a wall.
  • On each death, an inheritance screen offers a limited set of slots. You choose which traits and which skills cross into your heir.
  • Traits run body and mind: strength, resilience, long life, keen senses, temperament, resistance to disease.
  • Slots are scarce on purpose. You can't carry everything, so every inheritance sacrifices something you fought to build.
  • Across many lives you sculpt a line built for the world to come, choosing toward the traits the next age will demand.
  • Mutations, recessive traits, and rare lineages give a long run real surprise.Vision
VI

Building & Shelter · Two Masters

  • Crafting serves two masters at once: surviving today, and carrying the line forward.
  • For the present, build shelters and homes against the cold, the dark, the beasts, and the coming cataclysm.
  • Shelter grows with the age: a hide lean-to in the Stone years, timber longhouses, stone halls, and upward.
  • Some builds protect the body. Some protect the line. A strong home raises more children safely to inherit.
  • What you raise outlasts you. Your descendants inherit the walls, the hearth, and the home you built.
  • Settlements grow with your blood and become the seat of a dynasty.Vision

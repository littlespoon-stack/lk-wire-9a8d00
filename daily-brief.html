Sharper Tools

Use AI better, up to agents improving agents
MoneyInfluence
  • Self-improving agents are now a labs roadmap item, not a side paper. Anthropic is building a "dreaming" system where agents improve from their own runs, alongside long-running managed workflows. Google used I/O 2026 to declare an "agentic Gemini era" with Managed Agents in the Gemini API. The frontier Littlespoon wants to live on is now the headline feature at two of the biggest labs.
  • Hermes (Nous Research) is the open-source one to watch: it compiles successful task runs into permanent, reusable skill packages instead of wiping state at session end, and it self-hosts. Self-hosted sidesteps the June 15 metering for the parts we can run ourselves, and the skill-compilation pattern is exactly what our plugin and marketplace are built to hold.
  • The number that should reset your planning: METR finds the length of tasks agents can finish on their own has been doubling every seven months for years, and in 2024 to 2025 it accelerated to every four months. Whatever an agent cannot quite finish today, assume it can in a quarter.
  • The honest caveat, repeated because it matters: a self-improving agent is only as good as its evaluation loop. Reward shallow signals like output volume or speed and it gets confidently wrong at scale. Our next rung: a self-critique pass where one agent grades another's output against our own SOP loop before it reaches you. We already have "Convene the Collective" and the Concept Forge as the rubric. A weekend prototype, not a quarter.

The Home Front

Agents for family and personal life
Life
  • The family-assistant category keeps hardening. Nori, Cozi, Ohai, and Kora all ship the same spine: a household brain over shared calendars and lists that handles meals, carpools, chores, and school events, with low-friction capture by voice, photo, or email. Ohai's twist is a human in the loop for the messy items like detailed school schedules, AI does the routine and a person checks the tricky bits.
  • The pattern that earns its keep: these win when coordination load is high and every adult needs the same live truth. Shared calendar plus light AI drafting kills the highest-friction fights first, the "who is picking up who" ones.
  • Actionable: we already build a daily brief that pulls scattered inputs and tells you what matters by morning. Point that exact engine at home. A household brief that reads a shared calendar and a notes file and each morning says who is where, what is due, and what is running low. We own the template, so this is a config job, not a build, and it doubles as a live test bed for the same connector wiring the Roll-Up needs anyway. Low effort, direct quality-of-life return.

The Hustle

AI into money and influence, with first steps
MoneyInfluence
  • The money is in installing agents, not launching a unicorn. The AI agents market ran about $7.63B in 2025 and is projected near $183B by 2033. The fastest cash inside that is unglamorous: be the specialist who builds agentic workflows inside an existing business. Setup runs $500 to $1,500, monthly management $100 to $300, and workflow-automation retainers land at $1,000 to $8,000 a month. Five to eight retainer clients is a six-figure year.
  • The second lever is one we already half-built: a skills marketplace. AI agent skills sell at $50 to $200 one-time or $10 to $30 a month, and the market is early enough that first movers in a niche own it. We literally stood up a private plugin marketplace this week with 21 agents and 13 skills. The infrastructure to sell skills is already sitting in the repo.
  • Play one (this week): package one agentic workflow as a paid service. Pick a single high-pain, repeatable business task we already automate well. First step: write a one-page offer (the task, the before, the after, a flat setup price plus a monthly), then ask Vi to name three people in the network who run that exact task by hand. A free demo for one of them by next week.
  • Play two (compounding): turn the marketplace outward. The lilkat skills we built for ourselves are sellable to anyone running the same stack. First step: pick the two most reusable, generic skills we own, strip anything lilkat-specific, and list them as a paid pack. It costs almost nothing because the build already happened, and it plants the first stake in The Agency idea that is parked in the backlog.
